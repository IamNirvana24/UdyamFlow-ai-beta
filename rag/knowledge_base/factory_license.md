# Factory License
slug: factory_license
authority: Directorate of Factories, Department of Labour, Government of Punjab (Chief Inspector of Factories)
applies_to: Premises meeting the definition of a "factory" under the Factories Act, 1948 — broadly, manufacturing premises employing workers above a prescribed headcount, with a lower threshold where power is used.

## What it is
A licence to run a manufacturing premises as a registered factory, issued after the factory building plans and layout are approved by the Chief Inspector of Factories.

## Why it is required
The Factories Act governs worker safety, working hours, welfare facilities, and hazardous processes. Operating an unlicensed factory exposes the occupier and manager to prosecution and makes worker compensation claims far harder to defend.

## Two-stage process
1. **Approval of factory plans** — building layout, machinery placement, ventilation, and escape routes are submitted and approved before construction or installation.
2. **Grant of licence** — after the premises is built to the approved plan, the licence is issued naming the occupier and the manager.

## Typical documents required
- Approved building plan and factory layout drawing
- List of machinery with horsepower ratings and installed electrical load
- Details of manufacturing process and raw materials, flagging any hazardous process
- Number of workers to be employed, shift-wise
- Occupier and manager details with consent of the manager
- Proof of premises possession
- Fire safety and pollution clearances where the process requires them

## Position in the approval sequence
Comes after Building Plan Approval and Trade License, and generally alongside or before Fire NOC and Pollution NOC, since the inspectorate will look for those where the process warrants them.

## Practical notes
Licence fees scale with the number of workers and the installed horsepower, so a small unit pays substantially less than a large one. Renewal is periodic. The headcount thresholds that trigger the Act (with and without power) should be checked against the current text of the Act, as state amendments exist.
