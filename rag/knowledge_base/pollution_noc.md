# Pollution NOC (Consent to Establish / Consent to Operate)
slug: pollution_noc
authority: Punjab Pollution Control Board (PPCB), under the Water Act 1974 and the Air Act 1981
applies_to: Industrial units categorised as Red, Orange, or Green under the CPCB categorisation. White-category units are largely exempt from consent but may still need to intimate the Board.

## What it is
Two distinct consents, often spoken about as one:
- **Consent to Establish (CTE)** — permission to set up the unit at the proposed site, granted before construction or machinery installation.
- **Consent to Operate (CTO)** — permission to actually run the unit, granted after pollution control equipment is installed and verified.

## Why it is required
Discharging effluent or emitting air pollutants without consent is an offence under the Water and Air Acts and can result in closure directions and disconnection of utilities. Electricity connections for industrial units are frequently held up pending proof of consent.

## Category logic
Units are colour-categorised by pollution potential. Red-category processes face the longest scrutiny, the shortest consent validity, and the highest fees; Green-category units are processed far more quickly. The category depends on the manufacturing process, not on turnover.

## Typical documents required
- Detailed project report describing the manufacturing process
- Site plan and land documents
- Water balance chart — source, consumption, and effluent generation figures
- Details of effluent treatment plant and air pollution control equipment
- Hazardous waste generation details and disposal arrangement
- Capital investment figures, which determine the fee slab
- Environmental Clearance where the project attracts it

## Position in the approval sequence
CTE is one of the earliest approvals for any manufacturing unit and precedes construction. CTO comes near the end, before commercial production starts.

## Practical notes
Consent fees are based on capital investment slabs and category. Validity periods differ sharply by category, so a Red-category unit renews far more often than a Green one. Water consumption and effluent figures stated in the application become binding conditions.
