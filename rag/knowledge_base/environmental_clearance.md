# Environmental Clearance
slug: environmental_clearance
authority: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (Category A projects) or the State Environment Impact Assessment Authority, Punjab (Category B projects), under the EIA Notification 2006
applies_to: Only projects and activities listed in the schedule to the EIA Notification, above the specified thresholds. Most small and medium units do not attract it.

## What it is
Clearance granted after an assessment of the project's likely environmental impact, imposing binding conditions on how the project is built and operated.

## Why it is required
For scheduled projects it is a statutory precondition to construction. Starting work without it is treated as a violation and regularisation is difficult, expensive, and not guaranteed.

## The four-stage process
1. **Screening** — determining whether the project is Category A or B, and for B whether it needs full assessment (B1) or not (B2).
2. **Scoping** — the authority issues Terms of Reference defining what the impact study must cover.
3. **Public consultation** — public hearing at the site district, with written submissions considered. Some categories are exempt.
4. **Appraisal** — an Expert Appraisal Committee reviews the EIA report and recommends grant or rejection.

## Typical documents required
- Pre-feasibility report and detailed project report
- Environmental Impact Assessment report prepared by an accredited consultant
- Environment Management Plan with mitigation commitments
- Baseline environmental monitoring data for the site
- Land use certificate and site clearance
- Public hearing proceedings where applicable

## Position in the approval sequence
Precedes construction and precedes the Pollution Board's Consent to Establish for projects that attract it.

## Practical notes
This is the longest single approval in the chain — timelines run in months, not weeks, driven by the baseline monitoring season and the public hearing. Whether a project attracts EC at all is a threshold question that should be settled at the very start of project planning, because the answer reshapes the entire schedule.
